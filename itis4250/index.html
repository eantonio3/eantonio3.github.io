Hello, my name is Efren Antonio, and this site showcases my work in digital forensics through a series of lab reports completed as part of my coursework in ITIS 4250 - Computer Forensics at UNC Charlotte.

Each lab demonstrates different aspects of forensic analysis, from verifying the integrity of digital evidence and examining file systems, to recovering deleted files, analyzing metadata, and linking digital artifacts to real-world investigations.

My goal with this portfolio is to provide a clear, professional record of my forensic skills and methodologies, while also making the material accessible for others who are interested in learning about the field of digital forensics.

Lab 1

This lab involved analyzing two forensic thumb drive images using FTK Imager. I verified their integrity with MD5 hashes, confirmed they both used the FAT32 file system, and found they had the same size and nearly identical contents. The only difference was that one image had files in the SkypePortable folder while the other did not. Overall, the evidence suggests both images came from the same thumb drive.

Lab 2

This lab involved analyzing a forensic thumb drive image related to an investigation of stolen Apple devices. Using FTK Imager and Autopsy, I verified the image integrity, recovered deleted files, and found a photo of a Mac Mini with a distinctive sticker under the Apple logo. Metadata revealed the photo was taken with an iPhone 6s near Woodward Hall at UNC Charlotte. These findings suggest the photo could be tied to one of the stolen Apple devices.

Lab 3

This lab involved analyzing forensic images from Malcom Monarch's laptop and a hotel business center computer using FTK Imager, Autopsy, and Registry Explorer. I verified the integrity of the images with MD5 hashes and confirmed the systems' operating environments. The analysis revealed Monarch's email communications with criminal associates, web searches related to explosives, and use of CCleaner to conceal activity. I also uncovered downloaded images that revealed his appearance and research into forensic tools like FTK Imager. Overall, the evidence provides strong insight into Monarch's intent and attempts to cover his tracks.

Lab 4

This lab focused on examining a chat application database extracted from the phone of Rett Harring using SQLite Studio. I analyzed communication logs to identify key contacts, conversation timelines, and user details. Evidence included a stolen gold inlaid guitar pick, images of associates, and frequent messaging with a contact named “GypsyWoman.” I was also able to trace specific conversations such as the “Nu phone who dis” exchange and identify the participants by email. Overall, the database provided valuable forensic evidence about Harring's activities and connections.

Lab 5

This lab involved analyzing a forensic image of Eugene “Gene” Poole's desktop computer using FTK Imager, Autopsy, and Registry Explorer. I verified the integrity of the image with MD5 hashes and confirmed the system owner and organization details. The analysis uncovered a text file in the recycle bin referencing a possible hideout, records of USB devices connected to the system, and signs of office program usage. There was also evidence suggesting Poole's interest in hacking tools and possible links to drug-related activities near beach towns. Overall, the investigation connected Poole's computer usage to his criminal operations and potential locations of interest.
